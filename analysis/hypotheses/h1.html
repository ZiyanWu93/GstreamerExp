SCReAM vs GCC — what arrives at the viewer, and what the algorithm sent

main finding

At the viewer, the SCReAM-vs-GCC ranking depends on the network's pace: GCC delivers more frames on slow-changing networks; SCReAM delivers more on fast-changing networks. At the sender, SCReAM pushes more bytes onto the wire on both networks — but more bytes sent does not mean more frames received.

Two video congestion-control algorithms, SCReAM and GCC, sense the network differently and pace the encoder differently. Zhang et al. (Congestion Control for RTP Media: A Comparison on Simulated Environment, SIMUtools 2019) reported on sender-side behavior — what each algorithm tells the encoder to produce. This page reports both: what the algorithm sent (sender side) and what arrived at the viewer (receiver side). The two answer different questions, and they rank the algorithms in opposite directions on the slow-changing network.

sender result · why sender ≠ receiver

SCReAM tells the encoder to push at a much higher rate than GCC on both networks — far above the 300 kbps bottleneck during the squeeze. Most of those over-cap bytes get dropped at the bottleneck queue and never reach the viewer. Compare the same time window in the receiver plots above: the rate caps at 300 kbps regardless of what the sender attempted.

Glossary of terms used on this page

Network capacity
How many bits the network can carry per second. We test against a synthetic network whose capacity is known exactly.
Capacity-step
A network whose capacity drops abruptly (e.g. 5 Mbps → 300 kbps), holds, then jumps back. A stair, not a ramp.
Bottleneck phase
The window where capacity is at its lowest (300 kbps in our tests) — the part where the algorithm's choices matter.
Slow-changing network
Capacity holds each level for several seconds before flipping. Both algorithms have time to react and settle into a steady rate.
Fast-changing network
Capacity flips every 2 seconds. Neither algorithm gets time to settle.
Sender rate
How many bits per second the algorithm tells the video encoder to produce. This is what the algorithm controls; the plots show it over time.
Utilization
Sender rate ÷ network capacity. 1.0 = sender exactly fills the pipe. >1.0 = sender is over-pushing, packets pile up or drop. <1.0 = sender is leaving bandwidth unused.
Sawtooth
A pattern of climbing fast, hitting a wall, crashing down, and climbing again.
